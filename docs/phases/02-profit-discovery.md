# PHASE 2 — PROFIT DISCOVERY

## Objective
Replace assumptions with verified MJS operating data and identify where money, time, stock, and margin are being lost or delayed.

## Discovery domains
### Products
- SKU list
- pack weight/unit
- bulk or bale purchase cost
- packaging and label cost
- selling price
- retailer margin
- actual gross contribution

### Production
- bale/bulk input quantity
- actual usable yield
- rejected/waste quantity
- packing time
- actual unit cost

### Outlets
- outlet master list
- area
- visit cadence
- stock placed
- stock sold
- stock remaining
- returns
- collection behavior

### Routes
- normal route sequence
- distance/time approximation
- fuel cost
- other field expense
- outlets served
- contribution collected/generated

## Money-leak hypotheses
1. Too much field inventory.
2. Stockouts between visits.
3. Replenishment of slow/dead products.
4. Cash collection occurring too late.
5. Low-value outlet visits consuming route cost.
6. Margin blindness by SKU/outlet.
7. Packing yield variance.
8. Owner memory becoming the business bottleneck.

## Discovery workflow
Baseline → Observe → Record → Reconcile → Calculate → Validate.

## Evidence rules
- Real MJS data beats assumed thresholds.
- Observed stock must be distinguished from estimated stock.
- Historical records must remain attributable to source transactions.
- Missing data is recorded as an explicit gap, not silently imputed.

## Deliverables
- verified business model
- money-leak map
- baseline KPI sheet
- validated user journey
- initial rule candidates
- data-quality issue list

## Exit gate — DISCOVERY VALIDATED
Critical pricing, stock, collection, route and profitability rules can be traced to real evidence from MJS.
